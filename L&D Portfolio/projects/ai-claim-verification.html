HRBP AI Claim Verification

Help an HRBP spot an unsupported claim before it becomes an employee communication.

01 / Diagnose

Separate a skill gap from a workflow barrier.

Evidence: Fictional HR communication and four hypotheses requiring investigation.

In the fictional scenario, an HRBP uses AI to draft an invitation for an AI-awareness session. The draft adds “attendance is mandatory” and “every participant receives a certificate,” although neither statement appears in the supplied briefing.

If released unchecked, such claims could mislead employees and create avoidable clarification, rework and trust concerns. This is a plausible risk scenario, not a documented employer incident or measured financial loss.

The performance gap

Expected: source each checkable claim, correct unsupported assertions, escalate unresolved policy statements and retain human approval.

Current: no observed employee baseline or audited workplace drafts are available. Two seeded claims illustrate a failure mode, not an employee error rate.

Four hypotheses, each requiring evidence
HypothesisHow to check itResponse
Cannot distinguish claims from plausible wordingUncoached six-claim pre-testGuided practice and independent reassessment
No approved source, version or accessSource inventory and owner interviewsControlled source pack and access rules
Unclear approval or escalationMap the release workflowNamed approver and exception path
Time pressure discourages verificationReview cycle times and correction patternsReserve checking time in the workflow

My recommendation is conditional: if access or authority prevents verification, fix that control first. Training can build the checking skill, while the organisation must provide an authoritative source and a release owner.

02 / Design

Make the objective observable.

Evidence: Aligned objective, practice and proposed unseen assessment.

Proposed objective: in 20 minutes, independently classify 6/6 claims in an unseen fictional HR draft, cite the supported claims, correct 2/2 unsupported statements, escalate 1/1 unresolved policy claim and introduce zero invented replacements.

Practice: a 30-minute workshop with a five-minute worked example, 15-minute paired exercise and ten-minute feedback review. Learners use a claim → source/version → status → action job aid.

Independent assessment: an additional 20-minute task using a different draft, a locked answer key and a reviewer. Failed attempts receive targeted coaching and a new-case retest. Completion alone does not establish proficiency.

04 / Deliver

Prepare a controlled practice session.

Evidence: Proposed facilitation and review plan. No employee workshop has run.

The proposed sequence is a worked example, paired practice with a job aid and feedback, followed by an independent task using a different draft.

  • Facilitator: lead the 30-minute practice workshop and explain the verification process.
  • SME: validate the source pack, answer key and assessment rubric before use.
  • L&D coordinator: confirm access, prepare materials, schedule the separate 20-minute assessment and retain review records.

Workplace facilitation and SME sign-off remain pending. The web interaction demonstrates a teaching decision, not employee performance.

Proposed expansion gates, not achieved results

Agree the gates with the sponsor before starting: controls in place for all sampled tasks; at least 90% of learners pass the unseen test; at least 95% of audited workplace drafts comply, with an improvement of at least 15 percentage points unless baseline is already at least 85%; and zero critical claim escapes or data-handling incidents. These are proposed acceptance criteria. Simulation-only results cannot establish workplace transfer.

Primary measures are verified-draft compliance and critical misses. Secondary measures include independent assessment pass rate, factual corrections, checking/approval time and manager confirmation. Faster drafting should never compensate for weaker verification.

Use errors to guide the next revision

Classify each error before selecting an action. Missing sources or unclear authority require a process fix. Persistent verification errors require different practice, coaching and a fresh assessment. The sponsor decides whether workplace evidence supports expansion.

What still needs validation

An SME must approve the sources and rubric. A sponsor must authorise the pilot, tool use and data controls. Real baseline and workplace samples are required before reporting performance change or rework reduction.
